Solcoand Lexicon

Lexicon and Solco both sit between your downloads and the booth, and neither one mixes. Lexicon is built around your DJ apps: it reads one app's library, helps you clean it up and writes it into another. Solco is built around the tracks: it listens to each one, grids it, keys it, fixes its tags and writes the USB your Pioneer players read.

I make Solco, so read this with that in mind. Every line about Lexicon comes from Lexicon's own site and manual, listed under Sources with the date I checked it.

1What each is for

Two library apps, from two ends.

Lexicon
"Library management for professional DJs." It converts your library between rekordbox, Serato, Engine DJ, Traktor, VirtualDJ and djay Pro, and its FAQ says "you can't mix tracks in Lexicon". L1 L2 L11
Solco
Prepares new music for DJing on your computer: beatgrid and downbeats, key, loudness, style and character, tags and covers, duplicates and playlists. Then it writes the USB drive Pioneer CDJ and XDJ players read, or a Traktor collection. It imports from rekordbox, Traktor and Apple Music, and isn't a converter between DJ apps. S1 S2 S9

3Where Solco does more

Hearing each track.

  • Character from the audio. Solco estimates style, mood, vocals and a dozen other readings for every track on your computer, each searchable. S4 Lexicon measures energy from the audio and takes danceability, happiness and popularity from its online database. L4 L6
  • A gig planner. Describe the night and Solco's assistant proposes a folder of playlists from your own tracks, which you review before anything is created. S6
  • A short list of grids to check. Solco lists only the grids it isn't sure about, each with the reason, so you check a handful by ear. S3
  • No account. A licence key, entered once. S12 Lexicon needs a free account. L2
  • ARM Linux. Solco runs on ARM64 Linux as well as x86-64. S10 Lexicon says "There is no ARM version". L12

4Where Lexicon does more

Between DJ apps, and beyond.

  • Converting between DJ apps. If you play Serato, Engine DJ, VirtualDJ or djay Pro, Lexicon carries cues, loops and grids across. L11 Solco reaches those apps with plain playlists only. S11
  • Automatic cue points. Lexicon's Ultimate plan sets cues at drops, breakdowns and the fade-out. L8 Solco doesn't set cues for you. S14
  • Phones, cloud and streaming. A phone app, cloud backup, cloud storage for tracks and four streaming services. L14 L15 Solco has none of these.
  • Tracks to the players over the network. DeckConnect loads tracks onto CDJs and XDJs without a USB. L18
  • Older Macs. Lexicon runs from macOS Catalina; Solco needs 13.4 or later. L12 S10
  • It's established. Lexicon is on version 1.12. Solco is in private beta. L17

5Together

Using both.

I haven't tested Solco and Lexicon on the same library, so take this as a suggestion. Solco writes its keys, and the tags you accept, into the music files themselves, so they travel with the files into any app that reads tags. S15 S7

If your night is on Pioneer players, Solco's USB carries the grids, cues and waveforms. If you play another app, a converter like Lexicon is the tool for moving cues and grids into it. If both apps write to one stick, try it on a spare drive first.

Questions

What DJs ask about Solco and Lexicon.

Does Lexicon analyse tracks, or only move libraries?

Both. On its Essential and Ultimate plans, Lexicon analyses BPM, beat grids (including Fluid grids for tracks whose tempo changes), key and energy on your computer, as of November 2026.

Can either one make a CDJ USB without rekordbox?

Yes. Both write OneLibrary and the Device Library on the same drive, so the newest players and older ones list the tracks. Solco does it on every drive by default.More in the help centre

Is Solco a Lexicon alternative?

For preparing new tracks and writing Pioneer USBs, it can be. For moving a library between Serato, Engine DJ, Traktor, VirtualDJ and djay Pro with its cues and grids, no: that is what Lexicon is built for.

Do both run on Linux?

Yes. Lexicon supports 64-bit Intel and AMD Linux. Solco runs on x86-64 and ARM64 Linux, as DEB and RPM packages or a portable archive.More in the help centre
