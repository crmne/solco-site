Solcoand MIXO

MIXO calls itself "The Cloud DJ Library": it keeps one library and moves it between DJ apps, computers and phones. Solco keeps one library too, and listens to every track in it: grids, keys, character, tags, duplicates, then the USB your Pioneer players read.

I make Solco, so read this with that in mind. Every line about MIXO comes from MIXO's own site, listed under Sources with the date I checked it.

3Where Solco does more

Listening to each track.

  • Grids analysed from the audio. Solco grids every track itself, follows a live drummer through tempo changes, finds the downbeats and lists only the grids worth checking. S3 MIXO's guides describe grids built from the BPM and imported data, and editing them. X4
  • Character from the audio. Style, mood, vocals and a dozen other readings, each searchable. S4
  • Tags and covers looked up. Titles, artists, albums, years, genres and covers from the music catalogues, reviewed before anything is written. S7
  • Duplicates by sound. Solco compares the audio, so it finds an MP3 and a FLAC of one track under different names. S8 MIXO compares duration, title and artist. X11
  • A gig planner. Describe the night and the assistant proposes a folder of playlists from your own tracks, for you to review. S6
  • No account. A licence key, entered once. S12 MIXO needs an account to log in. X13

4Where MIXO does more

One library, everywhere.

  • Moving between DJ apps. MIXO exports your library to a dozen DJ apps. X5 Solco writes Pioneer USBs and Traktor collections, and plain M3U8 playlists for the rest. S11
  • Serato and OneLibrary imports. MIXO imports Serato 5 libraries, including their tempo changes, and OneLibrary drives. X16 X18 Solco doesn't import from Serato. S2
  • Phones and several computers, synced through your own cloud storage. X1 X13
  • Streaming playlists. Playlist Matcher finds a streaming service's playlist in your own library. X17
  • Older computers. MIXO runs on macOS from 10.11; Solco needs 13.4 or later. X13 S10

5Together

Using both.

I haven't tested Solco and MIXO on the same library, so take this as a suggestion. Solco writes its keys, and the tags you accept, into the music files themselves, so any app that reads tags sees them. S15 S7 If you keep a library in sync across several DJ apps, that is MIXO's job; if your night is on Pioneer players, Solco's USB carries its grids, cues and waveforms.

One rule if both write to the same drive: MIXO's desktop export merges with what's on it X12, and Solco's exports only add and update S9, but I haven't tried the two on one stick. Use a spare drive first.

Questions

What DJs ask about Solco and MIXO.

Can MIXO and Solco both write a CDJ USB?

Yes, both write the Device Library and OneLibrary without rekordbox. MIXO's USB export is on its Gold plan, a beta feature, for FAT32 drives of up to 999 tracks, as of November 2026. Solco writes both libraries on every drive, to FAT32, exFAT or, on a Mac, HFS+.More in the help centre

Is Solco a MIXO alternative?

For preparing tracks and writing Pioneer USBs, it can be. For keeping one library in sync across many DJ apps, computers and phones, no: that is what MIXO is built for.

Does either one upload my music?

MIXO says no music is held on its servers and syncs through your own Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox. Solco analyses on your computer, and your music never leaves it. Tag lookups and the assistant send track details to Solco's service only when you use them.More in the help centre

Can Solco import my MIXO library?

Not directly. Solco imports from rekordbox, Traktor and Apple Music, so a library MIXO has exported to one of those can come into Solco with its playlists and cues. I haven't tested that path.More in the help centre
