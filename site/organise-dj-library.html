Solco · Your library

A tidy library,step by step

A DJ library gets messy one download at a time: tags that say "Track 01", the same tune bought twice, a grid that drifts, a playlist you can't find on the night. Tidying it goes quicker in the right order, because each step uses what the one before it found.

These are the seven steps in Solco's sidebar, in the order Solco takes them, and what each one does to your music.

1Add music

Bring it in, where it is.

Point Solco at your music folders. Your files stay where they are, and with folder watching on, renames, moves and new files show up by themselves. Make your Downloads an incoming folder and new tracks wait apart from your library until you move them in. Already in rekordbox, Traktor or Apple Music? Your playlists, cues, ratings and colours come over, and the other app's library is only read.

2Analysis

Let it listen to everything.

Solco works through every track in the background: beatgrid and downbeats, key, loudness, waveform, the fingerprint it uses to recognise the track, and readings such as energy, style and vocals. It all runs on your computer, and you keep working while it does. Nothing here needs a decision from you.

3Tags

Fix the tags before anything else.

Solco recognises each track by its sound and its tags and proposes the right title, artist, album, label, year, genre and cover. Confident matches go in together; the rest wait for you, field by field. Nothing is written to a file until you apply it, and the audio is never touched.

4Duplicates

Then clear out the copies.

With the tags right, copies are easier to find and to tell apart. Solco finds the same recording by its sound, even an MP3 and a FLAC under different names, and lists tracks with the same title and artist separately. You choose the copy you keep. Extra copies go to the trash, and their cues and playlist entries move to the one you keep.

5Beatgrids

Check the few grids worth a look.

Most grids are ready as they are. Solco lists only the ones it isn't sure about, with the reason, such as maybe twice too fast, and a one-click fix. Tracks whose tempo moves, like a live drummer, get a grid that follows them.

How Solco finds beat grids, and how to fix one

6Playlists

Build playlists from a clean library.

Make playlists and folders by hand, or save a search as a smart playlist that fills itself as new music arrives, such as bpm:124..130 key:8A energy:>=60. The playlist assistant can plan a night as a folder of playlists from your own tracks, and you review every change first.

7Export

Tidy at home.
Tidy on stage.

Put it on the USB.

Pick your playlists and plug in a drive. Solco writes the USB Pioneer CDJ and XDJ players read, with grids, cues, waveforms and covers, in your playlists' order and folders. An export only adds and updates: nothing on the drive is removed.

Your first export, step by step
  • Back up first. Save a backup of your Solco library before big changes, and keep backups of your music, as you always should.
  • One way in. Let new music land in an incoming folder and move it into your library once it's tagged.
  • Searches, not piles. A smart playlist keeps itself up to date; a hand-made one keeps the order you chose.
  • Cues once. Set cues in Solco or on the player: the next export brings the player's back.

Questions

What DJs ask about organising.

In what order should I organise my DJ library?

Bring the music in, let it be analysed, fix the tags, remove duplicates, check the few uncertain grids, then build playlists and write the USB. Each step uses what the one before found: duplicates are easier to spot once titles and artists are right.More in the help centre

Do I have to move all my music into one folder?

No. Solco works with your music where it is and watches each folder you add. If you want it in one place, Gather into folder 1 moves every track into your main music folder, keeping the folders each track was in, and lists anything it can't move first.More in the help centre

Will Solco change my files without asking?

Tags from the catalogue and your own edits are written only when you apply them, through a checked copy of the file, and the audio is never touched. Solco does write the key it finds into each file's key tag, so other apps see the same key, and you can put your files' own keys back.More in the help centre

What happens to the duplicates I remove?

Nothing goes until you confirm a review that lists every file. Extra copies move to the trash on their own drive, so you can restore them, and their playlist entries and cues move to the copy you keep when the audio is the same.More in the help centre

Can I keep using rekordbox while I organise in Solco?

Yes. Solco only reads the rekordbox library, and one USB drive can go back and forth: rekordbox keeps Solco's playlists when it exports to the drive, and Solco keeps rekordbox's tracks, playlists and played sets.More in the help centre
