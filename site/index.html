2Beatgrid

Beatgrids that follow every tempo change.

Solco's local AI hears beats and bars in the audio itself. It locks onto a four-to-the-floor kick, finds the bars in a piano piece, and follows a live drummer through every tempo change, bar by bar. It gets almost every grid right: of the 9,434 tracks in my own library, 848 change tempo, and Solco asked me to check just 16 grids.

Real output, not a mock-up. Solco's analysis of four tracks from a DJ's library, drawn exactly as it comes out: every beat and bar line, and the tempo at each beat underneath.

4Character

Local AI figures out the energy, drive, dance and vocals.

Trained on how listeners rate music, it scores every track from 0 to 100, on your computer: Energy, Drive, Peak, Stability, Happy, Sad, Calm, Dance, Party, Edge and Vocals, and Mood if you add its column. Then search by all of it, together with key, BPM and genre, and find the tracks you forgot you had.

5Smart playlists

Playlists that sort themselves.

Save any search as a smart playlist: bpm:120..124, key:8A, energy:>=60. Every new track that fits files itself in. Or describe the night: the assistant looks through your library and proposes a folder of playlists, the parts of your set in playing order, for you to review before you create it. Or ask it to change a playlist you already have, and review the change before it is made.

7Duplicates

8On the USB

Plays on CDJs.
No rekordbox.

Solco writes the USB your CDJs and XDJs read directly.

Pick your playlists, plug in a drive, and Solco writes the rekordbox USB format that Pioneer players read. No laptop in the booth.

  • Everything on the USB. Grids, hot cues, memory cues, waveforms, artwork and playlists.
  • Only adds and changes. Nothing on the drive is removed.
  • Keeps your history. The sets you played on it stay.
  • Shows what is there. Tracks already on the drive are marked.
  • Cues come back. Cues you set on the player come into Solco with the next export.
  • Shares with rekordbox. Both can add to one drive, each keeping the other's playlists.
  • Solco's waveforms. CDJs and XDJs show Solco's own look, at each track's real level.

Questions

What DJs ask first.

Do I need rekordbox?

No. Solco writes the USB drive itself, in the rekordbox format Pioneer players read. If you still use rekordbox, Solco can import its library, and rekordbox keeps working as before. It is also much quicker at writing the drive: see the test.More in the help centre

Is Solco a rekordbox alternative?

For preparing your music, yes. Solco does the DJ library management, from beatgrids, keys and tags to duplicates and playlists, and writes the USB your CDJs and XDJs read, on macOS, Windows and Linux. It isn't for performing: you don't mix in Solco, you play from the USB on the players. If you still use rekordbox, both can add to the same drive, each keeping the other's playlists.More in the help centre

Can I still organise my library my own way?

Yes. Your folders, playlists, cues, grids and tags stay exactly as you set them. Solco adds its readings next to yours. It writes two things into your files by itself: an ID tag, so it can follow a file you move, and its key, so your other apps and players show the same key. Turn key writing off, or put your files' own keys back, in Settings › Analysis. Every other tag waits for you. Do as much by hand as you like, or none of it.More in the help centre

Which players read the drive?

Any Pioneer DJ or AlphaTheta player that reads the rekordbox Device Library (export.pdb), from the CDJ-2000 to the CDJ-3000, XDJ-XZ and XDJ-RX3. I have played Solco USBs on the XDJ-RX3, CDJ-2000NXS2, CDJ-2000 and XDJ-1000MK2. Players that read only the newer OneLibrary (the CDJ-3000X, XDJ-AZ, OPUS-QUAD and OMNIS-DUO) are not covered yet. Tell me what you play on when you sign up.More in the help centre

What comes over from my rekordbox library?

Playlists, folders, hot cues, memory cues, loops, ratings and colours. Beatgrids stay behind, because Solco grids every track itself. Your rekordbox library is only read, never changed.More in the help centre

Is my music uploaded?

No. Every analysis runs on your computer. The optional playlist assistant looks things up in your library step by step and proposes a plan, a folder of playlists you review before you create it, or a change to a playlist you already have, which you review before it is made. Sending it a message shares your request, the conversation, and the names and details of the tracks and playlists it looks up with Solco's service. Never audio or file paths.More in the help centre

What does it cost?

The beta is free, and your licence key renews itself while the beta lasts. New builds with fixes come out often. The price of the full release is not set yet; people on the waitlist hear it first.More in the help centre

Which computers does it run on?

macOS on Apple Silicon, Windows and Linux. Intel Macs are not supported.More in the help centre

What does private beta mean?

Solco is early, and it works only with a licence key made for you. Join the waitlist, and when there is a seat your personal key comes by email. Activate it once while online, on up to three of your computers; after that Solco works offline, and renews your key by itself while the beta lasts. I let DJs in small groups, read every message, and ship updates often. Keep a backup of your music, as you always should.More in the help centre

The name

tracciare il solco

Solco /ˈsol.ko/ is Italian for the groove of a record, and for the furrow ploughed before sowing.

Tracciare il solco means to prepare the way. So the music can grow, and you can play.
